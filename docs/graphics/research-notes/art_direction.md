# Art direction for a grim, grounded, painterly-photoreal medieval first-person look (and how small teams get it), as of November 2026

Note on method and evidence: 15 tool calls (searches plus fetches). Most of the art-director pages I fetched held less technical detail than their titles suggest: video interviews without transcripts, and GDC Vault sessions that are members-only. So the **Cited Findings** below are limited to what I actually confirmed. Long-standing practitioner techniques I know but did not re-check this session are kept under **Inferences** and labelled "[practitioner knowledge, not verified this session]". The report writer should treat them as well-known industry practice, not as sourced quotes.

## 1. Which shipped games best match the look, and what their art leads say

### Takeaway
The closest matches to the three concept images (overcast autumn square, thorn-and-wool gate, harbour mole) are Kingdom Come: Deliverance II for grounded medieval towns, A Plague Tale for grim, warm-lit southern-French medieval streets, and The Witcher 3 (Novigrad/Velen, and Beauclair in Blood and Wine) for dense, layered cities. Dishonored is the model for "painterly, not photoreal": a hand-made, stylised art direction with its own design language. Its leads talk about craft, restraint and selective detail more than about rendering features.

### Cited Findings
- A Plague Tale: Requiem's art director is Olivier Ponsonnet (Asobo Studio). The game is set in 14th-century south-east France. The team spent "more than three years" on visuals and aimed for "even more variety, darkness and beauty than we did for Innocence" (ArtStation Magazine, Jan 2023). — [ArtStation Magazine: A Plague Tale: Requiem Art Blast](https://magazine.artstation.com/2023/01/asobo-studio-a-plague-tale-requiem-art-blast/)
- Dishonored (2012) was built by hand rather than procedurally. Arkane recruited French traditional artists who drew, painted and sculpted by hand, and a painter known for hand-painting faces made the first character designs. — [GamesBeat: How Dishonored's artists created an oppressive world](https://gamesbeat.com/how-dishonoreds-artists-created-an-oppressive-world-from-scratch/)
- Dishonored was deliberately "neither a dark medieval setting nor photorealistic." Viktor Antonov: "I created a whole new design language." He described its industrial look as having "no rivets, no brass, no copper, and no tubes." On invented worlds: "The hard thing about designing a nonexistent place is that it may not be appealing if it is too strange." — [GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/how-dishonoreds-artists-created-an-oppressive-world-from-scratch/)
- Sébastien Mitton (art director, Dishonored 1 and 2) said "London felt oppressive for a French man." The team drew on smoggy London references and on films such as Brazil, Metropolis and Blade Runner. — [GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/how-dishonoreds-artists-created-an-oppressive-world-from-scratch/)
- Selective detail: Dishonored's team built story-relevant parts of the city in fine detail and left other areas unbuilt. — [GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/how-dishonoreds-artists-created-an-oppressive-world-from-scratch/)
- Mitton brought "the pillars of the Dishonored universe" to Dishonored 2's Karnaca and rejected the plain "steampunk" label (Game Informer video feature, May 2016; the technical detail is in the video, not the text). — [Game Informer: Oppression, Opulence and Decay](https://gameinformer.com/b/features/archive/2016/05/23/oppression-opulence-and-decay-inside-dishonored-2s-bold-art-direction.aspx)
- The Witcher 3: Blood and Wine's capital Beauclair was the subject of GDC 2017's "Building Beauclair: Capital City in The Witcher 3: Wild Hunt – Blood and Wine" (Kacper Niepokolczycki, CD PROJEKT RED). The talk covers "efficient ways of using available budgets for textures, meshes and other game elements" in the game's most demanding area, and how technical and environment art work together. The full talk is members-only. — [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1023965/Building-Beauclair-Capital-City-in)
- The Witcher 3's rendering was presented at SIGGRAPH ("The rendering features of The Witcher 3: Wild Hunt", Török and Krzyścin) and at GDC ("The Witcher 3: Enabling Next-Gen…"). — [SIGGRAPH History](https://history.siggraph.org/learning/the-rendering-features-of-the-witcher-3-wild-hunt); [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1020664/The-Witcher-3-Enabling-Next)
- Kingdom Come: Deliverance II has an official artbook ("The Art of Kingdom Come: Deliverance II"). Environment work included castles, villages and even chicken coops, built by artists such as Tomáš Duchek. — [Warhorse store](https://kingdomcome-store.com/en/Artbooks/The-Art-of-Kingdom-Come-Deliverance-II-Czech-version.html); [UWB news](https://info.zcu.cz/Jiri-Frei-is-the-new-candidate-for-Dean-of-the-Faculty-of-Health-Care-Studies/clanek.jsp?id=8016)
- Warhorse grew "from 35 people to 250" during development. KCD2's grounded realism is a large-team result, not a small-team one. — [GodIsAGeek interview, Aug 2024](https://godisageek.com/2024/08/kingdom-come-deliverance-ii-interview-when-i-started-we-had-35-people-now-we-have-250)
- Petr Susanka's ArtStation project "Nebakov Fortress – Kingdom Come: Deliverance II" is a direct reference for grounded Bohemian stone and timber architecture. — [ArtStation](https://petrsusanka.artstation.com/projects/nJ1389)

### Inferences
- The owner's target sits between KCD2 and Dishonored. KCD2 supplies the grounded, historically plausible architecture and the overcast autumn light. Dishonored supplies the painterly finish and a confident, slightly exaggerated design language. A small browser team should borrow Dishonored's method (hand-authored, stylised textures, a strong design language, selective detail), because photoreal density on the KCD2 scale needed a 250-person studio.
- Antonov's "no rivets, no brass…" is a transferable rule: define the world's look partly by what is banned. For Aethelgard that could read: no neon, no saturated primaries outside the HUD vials, and cyan-violet allowed only for glass and resonance.
- Dishonored's "fine detail only where the story is" fits a browser budget: detail the square, gate and mole densely, and keep everything past mid-distance as simple silhouette cards.
- Other games named in the brief (Thief, Elden Ring towns, Blasphemous, Dark and Darker, Enshrouded, Manor Lords) were not researched this session. See Gaps.

### Gaps

- No art-lead quotes found for KCD2 on lighting or colour. The artbook exists but is not online.

## 2. Lighting and colour for overcast scenes

### Takeaway
I found no fetchable primary source this session with numbers (key/fill ratios, grading LUTs) for these games. The established approach is still clear: a soft, cool, low-contrast sky key with no hard sun; warm, low-intensity practical lights (lanterns, windows) as the only saturated warm notes; strong aerial perspective that shifts distant objects toward the sky colour; and wet surfaces sold by roughness variation and reflections, not by darkening alone.

### Cited Findings
- Lighting is an environment artist's main tool for drawing players into a space. Lighting, haze and FX add depth and mood, and lighting and materials are inseparable: "If the material isn't set up properly it just won't show off well" (Josh Lynch, Infinity Ward/Raven, 80.lv, 21 Jan 2016, about 10 years old). — [80.lv: 5 Rules of Environment Design](https://80.lv/articles/5-rules-of-environment-design)
- A CD PROJEKT RED artist gave an interview on The Witcher 3's weather and lighting system. Weather-driven lighting is central to the look of Velen and Novigrad. — [DualShockers](https://www.dualshockers.com/the-witcher-3-wild-hunt-artist-talks-about-weather-lighting-consoles-graphics-and-much-more/)
- Players have repeatedly modded Witcher 3's lighting back toward the darker, more desaturated look of the 2013 VGX trailer. This suggests the grim overcast grade is what many players read as "concept art" quality. — [GameGPU on the VGX lighting mod](https://en.gamegpu.com/news/igry/modifikatsiya-vgx-lighting-vozvrashchaet-pervonachalnyj-mrachnyj-stil-the-witcher-3); [Nexus Mods](https://nexusmods.com/witcher3/mods/6501)

### Inferences [practitioner knowledge, not verified this session]
- **Key/fill for overcast:** overcast daylight is a big soft source from above, so the "key" is the sky hemisphere and the ground bounce is the fill. A key-to-fill ratio of about 2:1 to 3:1 reads as a dull day. Contrast comes from ambient occlusion in crevices (eaves, doorways, under market tents), not from cast shadows. In three.js this means a HemisphereLight (cool grey-blue sky, warm brown-olive ground) plus a weak, broad DirectionalLight with very soft shadows, plus baked or screen-space AO.
- **Warm against cool:** the concept images use the classic split of cool-grey daylight (about 6500–8000 K, desaturated blue-grey) against warm practicals (about 2000–2700 K, orange-amber lanterns and windows). Keep the practicals small, few and high in saturation so they become focal points. Point lights with short range plus an emissive window texture are cheap in a browser. Bake their glow into lightmaps or vertex colour where possible.
- **Grading:** a slight teal-grey lift in the shadows, warm highlights, about 10–20% global desaturation and a gentle filmic tonemapper (ACES or AgX in three.js) give the painterly cohesion. One 3D LUT applied in the final pass is cheap in WebGL and WebGPU. Keep the autumn oak gold as the most saturated natural colour in frame.
- **Aerial perspective:** distance fog tinted to the sky colour (not grey-black) with increasing desaturation flattens hills and the castle into value layers: foreground darkest and most saturated, background lightest and bluest. This "value staging" is what makes a real-time frame read as a painting, and it replaces the depth of field the owner rejected. Exponential height fog, denser low in valleys, is a cheap shader addition.
- **Wet cobbles:** wetness reads through lower roughness (0.1–0.3 in puddles), darker albedo (about 20–30% darker), puddle masks from a height map or vertex paint (water collects in the gaps between stones first), and something bright to reflect, such as warm lanterns or sky. Screen-space reflections or a planar or cube-map reflection for puddles make the lanterns double in the ground, which is the single most "concept-art" cue in the square image. Without anything to reflect, wet surfaces just look dark.
- **Hush/lore fit:** a muffled world suits low-contrast, soft light. Reserve the only high-contrast, high-saturation glow for cyan-violet resonance, so magic visually "breaks" the hush.

- No primary-source numbers (key/fill ratios, colour temperatures, LUT settings) from any shipped game's lighting artist were confirmed this session.

## 3. Set dressing and density: lived-in streets, decals, vertex painting, procedural placement

### Takeaway
"Lived-in" comes from props that tell a story about function (who works here, what happened), clutter that gathers where people and weather would leave it (edges, corners, doorways, under eaves), and a layered grime/moss/wetness pass. That pass is done with decals and vertex-painted blend masks rather than unique textures. Procedural placement works if it follows the same rules: placement driven by edges and curvature, not uniform scatter.

### Cited Findings
- Strong props for set dressing "go a long way to sell narrative and story". Spaces should "fit in the world and serve a purpose" and feel functional and grounded, so players are not confused (Josh Lynch, 80.lv, 2016). — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/5-rules-of-environment-design)
- Reference gathering ("Go outside!") is one of the five rules: observing real natural and man-made environments either teaches something new or reinforces what you know. — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/5-rules-of-environment-design)
- 80.lv has a dedicated breakdown on layered environment storytelling in 3D (not fetched). — [80.lv: Layered Environment Storytelling in 3D](https://80.lv/articles/layered-environment-storytelling-in-3d)
- KCD2's reactive, lived-in world design is covered by 80.lv (not fetched in full). — [80.lv: KCD2 brings reactive world](https://80.lv/articles/kingdom-come-deliverance-2-brings-reactive-world-more-ethnicities)

### Inferences [practitioner knowledge, not verified this session]
- **Clutter rules:**
  1. Clutter collects at boundaries (wall bases, doorsteps, stall edges, the well's rim), never in the middle of walkways.
  2. Group props in odd-numbered clusters (3 or 5) with one "hero" prop and supporting small props.
  3. Repeat a few props in varied rotation, scale (±10%) and tint, rather than adding many unique ones.
  4. Every cluster should answer "who uses this?" (the cooper's barrels by his door, fish crates on the mole, wool bales by the hedge gate).
  5. Leave negative space for readability and navigation.
- **Decals and grime layers:** a reusable decal atlas (water stains, soot above chimneys and lanterns, moss in north-facing corners, mud splash on the bottom 30–50 cm of walls, horse dung and straw on cobbles, leaf litter under the oak) adds history without unique textures. In three.js, DecalGeometry or projected-decal shaders work. For a browser, prefer baking decals into a second UV or vertex colour on static geometry.
- **Vertex painting:** paint per-vertex masks (R = moss, G = wetness, B = dirt/grime, A = damage or edge wear) and blend tiling materials in the shader. One mesh then gets many looks. This is the standard way small teams get variety from a small material library, and it is cheap on mobile GPUs.
- **Procedural rules:** derive placement from geometry. Use curvature and AO for moss and grime, world-space up-vector for wet and leaf accumulation, and distance-to-wall for clutter scatter. Use Poisson-disc sampling with exclusion zones (doorways, paths) rather than random scatter. Seed placement per house or stall from the "owner/trade" so props stay coherent (a cooper's house always spawns barrels and hoops).
- **Instancing:** cobbles, crates, barrels, roof tiles and rope coils suit InstancedMesh/BatchedMesh in three.js, which keeps draw calls low enough for a dense square in the browser.

## 4. Silhouettes and readability in first person

### Takeaway
I found no fetchable primary source this session. The established practice: vary rooflines (height, pitch, chimney rhythm, overhangs), place one tall landmark per vista (chapel tower, golden oak, castle) that the player can always orient by, and frame views with foreground "portals" (gate arch, tent edges, house corners). In first person the camera is always at eye height, so these vertical cues matter more than in third person.

### Cited Findings
- Dishonored built story-relevant sections in fine detail and left others unbuilt. This implies deliberate framing of what the player sees. — [GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/how-dishonoreds-artists-created-an-oppressive-world-from-scratch/)
- Lighting is used to lure players into a space and encourage exploration, which works as a readability and wayfinding tool. — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/5-rules-of-environment-design)

### Inferences [practitioner knowledge, not verified this session]
- **Roofline variety:** in a street of half-timbered houses, alternate gable-end-to-street and eave-to-street, vary storey count (2–4), jetty overhangs and chimney placement. A flat or regular skyline reads as "procedural" immediately. Keep slate roofs darker and cooler than walls, so the skyline cuts cleanly against an overcast sky.
- **Landmarks (the "weenie" principle from theme-park design):** one tall element per space draws the eye and aids navigation. In the square concept that element is the golden oak (warm, saturated, unique), with the chapel and its green banner as the secondary one and the castle on a hill as the distant third. Place landmarks where streets terminate (end-of-vista).
- **Value grouping:** read the frame in three to four value bands (dark foreground framing, mid-value street, light hazy background). Grade or fog toward this. A greyscale screenshot check is the quickest readability test.
- **Framing in first person:** start the player at composed viewpoints (entering through the gate, arriving at the mole). Use doorways, arches and tent canopies as foreground frames. Remember the viewmodel and HUD already take the bottom third of the screen, so put compositional interest at or above the horizon line.
- **Distant silhouettes in the browser:** the castle and hills can be low-poly or billboard cards with fog-matched colour. Silhouette shape matters more than detail at range.

### Gaps
- No primary sources on first-person-specific landmark design were fetched (for example GDC talks on Dishonored level-design/art balance; the PC Gamer article "Balancing art direction and level design in Dishonored 2" exists but was not fetched — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/balancing-art-direction-and-level-design-in-dishonored-2)).

## 5. First-person viewmodel design (hands, weapon, magic limb)

### Takeaway
Shipped first-person games render arms and weapons with a separate field of view (and often a separate pass) so animators can author at a fixed FOV while the world FOV changes. The viewmodel must be lit by the same environment lighting as the world, or it looks pasted on. Dishonored's marked left hand is the canonical "one hand is magic" design, which matches the owner's cyan glass arm.

### Cited Findings
- Many first-person games render arms and weapons at a separate FOV, so animators can "animate at a particular FOV" while designers or players change the camera FOV (Unreal Engine forum, 28 June 2021, linking a tutorial by Sahil Dhanju dated 20 June 2021). — [Unreal Engine Forums](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/render-your-first-person-arms-weapons-at-a-separate-fov-than-your-camera-fov/237944)
- Separate-FOV viewmodels in a deferred engine and Panini projection for first-person weapons are long-running developer topics. — [GameDev.net](https://gamedev.net/forums/topic/605666-first-person-weapon-with-different-fov-in-deferred-engine/4831659/); [Unreal Forums: Panini projection](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/how-to-implement-panini-projection-for-first-person-weapon-separate-fov-for-weapon/106976/3); [Polycount: 1st person FOV](https://polycount.com/discussion/34126/1st-person-fov)
- A common bug: FOV sliders not affecting the viewmodel, which happens exactly because it is a separate camera or projection. — [Bugnet](https://bugnet.io/blog/how-to-fix-fov-slider-not-affecting-the-weapon-viewmodel)
- Dishonored's arm/hand concept art, including the Outsider's mark on the left hand, is documented on the Dishonored Wiki. — [Dishonored Wiki: Arm Artifact Concept](https://dishonored.fandom.com/wiki/File:Arm_Artifact_Concept.png)

### Inferences [practitioner knowledge, not verified this session]
- **In three.js:** render the viewmodel with a second PerspectiveCamera (FOV about 50–60° against a world FOV of 75–90°) after clearing depth only (`renderer.autoClear = false; renderer.clearDepth()`), or put it on its own layer. This avoids the hand clipping into walls and keeps its proportions stable.
- **Matching the scene light:** the viewmodel must sample the same environment map, hemisphere light and fog colour as the world. Add a cheap "local light probe": tint the viewmodel's ambient by the nearest practical light (warmer near lanterns, cooler in open square). A mismatched viewmodel is the most common reason a first-person frame stops looking like concept art.
- **Framing and scale:** the hands sit in the lower-left and lower-right thirds, covering about 15–25% of the screen at rest. The weapon angles inward toward screen centre, its tip below the crosshair line. Keep the sword blade's value and hue distinct from the cobbles (bright steel edge against dark ground). Idle sway and breathing (1–2 px) make the hands feel alive. Avoid big bob, which adds visual noise the owner dislikes.
- **The glass arm (Dishonored/Skyrim/Ghostrunner lineage):**
  - Use a fresnel-rim emissive (cyan-to-violet), refraction or a screen-space "behind" sample with a faint chromatic offset, and an internal "veins" or "caustic" scrolling texture.
  - Pulse it with in-game sound events: louder means brighter. That ties directly to the lore, where sound is the predator's sense and resonance is the glow.
  - Use a very small point light attached to the arm to throw cyan onto nearby walls, so the magic limb lights the world. This is a cheap, powerful realism cue.
  - Keep glow tight, with no screen-wide bloom, to respect the owner's rejection of busy effects.
  - Skyrim tints the spell hand with a per-school colour and particle glow. Dishonored keeps its mark subtle until a power is used.

### Gaps
- No primary art-lead sources on Dishonored's hand design, Skyrim's magic hands or Ghostrunner's arm were fetched.

## 6. Ornate fantasy HUD in HTML/CSS

### Takeaway
Ornate frames in HTML are built with CSS `border-image` 9-slice: a single bronze frame image whose corners stay fixed while edges stretch or repeat. Game UI middleware (Coherent Gameface) uses the same 9-slice, clipping and masking model. Free CC0-style fantasy border sets exist for prototyping. Readability at phone size needs fewer, larger elements and a decorative-versus-functional split.

### Cited Findings
- Coherent Labs (Gameface, HTML/CSS UI middleware used in shipped games) documents 9-slice, clipping and masking as the core techniques for "advanced UI shapes", including a nine-slice modal tutorial. — [Coherent Labs guide](https://guide.coherent-labs.com/layout-assets-and-styling/graphics--shapes/advanced-ui-shapes/); [Nine-slice modal tutorial](https://assets.coherent-labs.com/blog/uitutorials/nine-slice-modal/)
- CSS `border-image` generators and guides show 9-slice scaling for UI stretching. — [CSS Portal border-image generator](https://www.cssportal.com/css-border-image-generator/); [BestHub: CSS 9-slice with border-image](https://besthub.dev/articles/master-css-9-slice-scaling-with-border-image-for-seamless-ui-stretching-b267f5ebeceb)
- Kenney's "Fantasy UI Borders" pack is available on itch.io and OpenGameArt as a prototyping source. — [Kenney on itch.io](https://kenney-assets.itch.io/fantasy-ui-borders); [OpenGameArt](https://opengameart.org/content/fantasy-ui-borders)
- Roblox's documentation also uses 9-slice for custom UI borders, confirming it is the cross-engine standard. — [Roblox Creator Docs: 9-slice](https://create.roblox.com/docs/en-us/ui/9-slice.md)

### Inferences [practitioner knowledge, not verified this session]
- **Building the bronze bar:**
  - `border-image: url(bronze-frame.webp) 48 fill / 48px / 0 round;` for the frame.
  - Layered `background` gradients (a dark verdigris-to-umber base, a top highlight `linear-gradient` and an inner shadow with `box-shadow: inset`) fake cast metal without large textures.
  - SVG filters (`feTurbulence` plus `feDisplacementMap` or `feSpecularLighting`) can add hammered-metal grain procedurally.
  - Keep the ornament in the corners and centrepieces (compass rose, weapon slot). Plain edges tile cleanly.
- **Vial gauges:** an SVG vial mask over a CSS-variable-driven fill height (`--hp: 0.72`), a glass highlight overlay and a slow liquid wobble. Keep the red, green and blue clearly separated in lightness too, not only in hue, so colour-blind players can tell them apart, and pair each with an icon and an optional number.
- **Phone readability:**
  - Collapse the bar to the essential items: 3 vials, weapon and compass. Move CHAR, INV, BOND and LOG into one menu button below about 600 px width.
  - Use a minimum touch target of 44–48 CSS px and frames scaled in `rem`/`clamp()`.
  - Use bronze trim at 8–12 px on mobile against 20–32 px on desktop.
  - Labels should use a readable serif with small caps (for example Cormorant, EB Garamond or IM Fell from Google Fonts) at 14 px or more, with text shadow or backplate for contrast against the 3D scene.
- **Comparable games:**
  - Diablo IV's HUD is a central ornate bar with two globe gauges.
  - Baldur's Gate 3's bottom hotbar uses restrained gilded frames.
  - Pentiment's UI is diegetic manuscript-style.
  - Darkest Dungeon's frames are heavy, gothic and chunky.
  - All of them keep ornament around the edges and the functional content plain. The Game UI Database (gameuidatabase.com) holds screenshot references for these titles.
- **Accessibility:**
  - Offer HUD scale, an opacity/minimal-HUD toggle and high-contrast text.
  - Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` for the vial wobble.
  - Make the HTML buttons real `<button>`s with labels, so they can be reached by keyboard and screen reader.

### Gaps
- The Game UI Database and Interface In Game pages for Diablo IV, BG3, Pentiment and Darkest Dungeon were not fetched this session. Those game comparisons come from general knowledge, not citations.
- No art-lead quotes on these HUDs were found.

## 7. Building an art bible so mixed asset sources look consistent

### Takeaway
I found no fetchable primary source this session. The standard art-bible lock-down list is a value and colour palette (with the "reserved colour" rules), a texel density target, a shared material library (PBR value ranges), a prop list with scale references, a lighting and grade reference, and a design-language list of allowed and banned motifs. That last item is what Antonov describes as creating "a whole new design language", including what the world does not use.

### Cited Findings
- Antonov described creating "a whole new design language" for Dishonored, defined partly by exclusions ("no rivets, no brass, no copper, and no tubes"). — [GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/how-dishonoreds-artists-created-an-oppressive-world-from-scratch/)
- Harmony of scene, props and materials is what makes a space beautiful, and badly set-up materials undermine the lighting. — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/5-rules-of-environment-design)
- CD PROJEKT RED's Beauclair talk centres on managing budgets for textures and meshes across a dense city. A browser project faces the same constraint, more severely. — [GDC Vault 2017](https://gdcvault.com/play/1023965/Building-Beauclair-Capital-City-in)

### Inferences [practitioner knowledge, not verified this session]
- **What to lock down:**
  1. **Palette:** about 12–16 swatches. Earth tones (umber, ochre, slate grey, moss green, oak gold) for the world, warm amber only for practicals, cyan-violet only for resonance/glass, and red, green and blue only for the HUD vials.
  2. **Value ranges:** albedo kept within roughly sRGB 30–240, no pure black or white, so the overcast light doesn't crush detail.
  3. **Texel density:** a single target, for example 512 px/m for hero props and 256 px/m for buildings in a browser game. Check imported assets (Kenney, Quaternius, Poly Haven, KitBash, AI-generated) with a checker texture and rescale UVs to match. Mismatched texel density is the main thing that makes mixed-source assets look "kit-bashed".
  4. **Material library:** around 10–20 tiling PBR materials (cobble, slate, oak timber, daub/plaster, rough stone, wet stone, wool, rope, iron, bronze). Every asset references these, plus the vertex-paint blend, instead of carrying unique textures.
  5. **Prop list and scale sheet:** door 2.0–2.2 m, barrel about 0.9 m, crate 0.6 m, market tent 3 m. A scale-reference human in every scene.
  6. **Shape language:** rounded, sagging, hand-made medieval forms (no perfectly straight timbers). Thorn and wool for the hedge gate.
  7. **Lighting and grade:** one shared LUT, fog colours per biome (town, gate and harbour).
- **Unifying pass for mixed assets:** run every imported asset through the same shader (shared triplanar grime, the same tonemapper and LUT, the same fog), and optionally through a light painterly post-filter (Kuwahara or a soft edge-darkening outline). This post pass is what turns photoreal-ish assets into a "painted" look without blur, and Kuwahara filters run in real time in WebGL. The owner rejected depth of field and blur, so test that a subtle Kuwahara doesn't read as blur. Edge-preserving filters usually don't.
